THE HEADLINE MECHANIC
Grass has height, not just presence.
Every tile is 0–3. A weak mower shaves one level per pass; a good one takes all three. So the early game is honestly slow rather than artificially slow, and every upgrade is a visible jump rather than a number.

Rentals and boosts
Rent a Zero-Turn for two minutes at R$ 49 — the flagship impulse buy. Rent one you own and it converts to 2× cash.
ROBUX · FOREVER
Passes and unlocks
Handed back at the start of every future run. A head start, not a bigger number.
THE FLOOR
Buy the Zero-Turn outright and you still clear every section, still race the same clock, still cannot skip a wave. What you bought is the first ten minutes back. Free loops — daily streak, playtime cash, a free rental every thirty minutes — exist so progression never depends on spending.
BUILT, NOT PLANNED
Tiles are data, not Instances.
The lawn lives in a buffer, one byte per tile — two bits of height, three of debris type, one wet flag. The server creates no part for it. Cutting rasterises the tool's swath with grid maths: no raycasts, no physics, no Touched events.
The client reports only where it is and what it holds. Every cut, sale and purchase is decided server-side, so exploit resistance falls out of the shape rather than being bolted on.

HOW BALANCE IS DECIDED
A simulator, and it runs in CI.
It plays an ROI-optimal player through every world from nothing and reports whether each is beatable inside its clock, where a run stalls, and what gear you finish holding. Pass a player count to check wave scaling.
WHAT IT CAUGHT
A section full of debris you cannot afford the tool for is a dead run. The simulator walled itself twice before bare hands can shift anything became a rule.
KNOWN WEAKNESS
Difficulty comes from length and complexity, not from time pressure tightening. Richer payouts made later worlds faster, so the fix is more ground.
